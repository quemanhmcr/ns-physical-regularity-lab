# Square-root bridge from productive source Reynolds to null-catalyst circulation

Let a productive core of radius `epsilon` carry strain amplitude `s`. Its source Reynolds is

`R_s=s epsilon^2/nu`.

The linear null catalyst `omega=A diag(2,-1,-1)x` generates the same axisymmetric productive ray at rate

`J=A^2 epsilon^2/7`.

Balancing the viscous maintenance demand `s nu/epsilon^2` gives

`A^2=7 s nu/epsilon^4`.

The actual Stokes circulation of one positive catalyst cap at radius `epsilon` is

`Gamma_cat=4pi A epsilon^3/(3sqrt3)`.

Therefore exactly

`Gamma_cat/nu = [4pi sqrt7/(3sqrt3)] sqrt(R_s)`.

This removes the apparent unit-core escape. At fixed unit strain the catalyst circulation can vanish as the core shrinks, but a singular high-Re productive core requires the catalyst circulation Reynolds to diverge.

The bridge is not an energy contradiction: the catalyst energy inside the core is only

`E_core=(4pi/15)R_s nu^2 epsilon`.

Its role is ancestry bookkeeping: a transaction-null nonlinear maintenance mechanism still acquires large physical circulation when asked to support the actual high-Re singular branch.
