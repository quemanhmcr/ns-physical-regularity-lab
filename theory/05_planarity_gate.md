# Planarity gate: closed vortex geometry before norms

Physics-first question: can a closed vortex loop create tangential self-stretching while remaining planar?

For any planar closed filament X(s), the Biot-Savart source element dX' and displacement X-X' both lie in the same plane. Their cross product is normal to that plane. Hence the self-induced velocity on the filament is normal to the plane. Because the tangent is in the plane, t . partial_s u = 0. Therefore a planar vortex filament may translate or bend out of the plane, but it has zero instantaneous material-line stretching from its own Biot-Savart field.

This is stronger than circular symmetry. Ellipses and arbitrary planar closed shapes obey the same geometric gate.

The next physically admissible route to self-stretching is genuinely three-dimensional geometry. We probe a family

X(theta) = (R cos theta, R sin theta, eps R sin(m theta)).

The m=1 member is still exactly planar (it lies in z = eps y), providing a null control. Modes m>=2 are genuinely nonplanar.

We measure the line-element stretching rate

sigma = t . (partial_s u) = X_theta . u_theta / |X_theta|^2

from the regularized Biot-Savart field without replacing the tensor kernel by absolute values. The dimensionless efficiency is

A = 4 pi R^2 sigma_rms / Gamma.

The experiment asks whether A turns on continuously from zero as nonplanarity eps is introduced, and at which order in eps.
