# The missing P1 channel

The certified higher response fills every compatible null Hodge family observed so far except `P1`.  This is potentially physical rather than accidental.

For a homogeneous divergence-free vector field of degree `d`, write on the sphere

`V=h n + T`,

with `div_S T=-(d+2)h`.  A poloidal `l=1` scalar potential `psi=a dot n` gives

`V_P1 = [2/(d+2)](a dot n)n + grad_S(a dot n)`.

Its sphere mean is

`<V_P1> = 2(d+3)/[3(d+2)] a`,

whereas every higher poloidal/toroidal angular sector has zero sphere mean.  Thus the sphere-mean vorticity is an intrinsic detector of `P1`.

The natural tangent Hodge velocity carrying this `P1` vorticity is a toroidal `l=1` swirl.  Its direction also carries nonzero fluid angular momentum, so `P1` is the natural rotational-momentum channel of the Hodge decomposition.

Module 140 attacks whether its absence is merely a consequence of the symmetric coupled root.  It evaluates higher null emissions for deliberately nonsymmetric feedback states and checks the sphere mean directly, while an explicit `P1` field serves as a sensitivity control.

If `P1` remains absent generically, the next proof task is to derive the exact Noether/torque identity responsible for this selection rule.  Until that derivation is complete, the angular-momentum interpretation is a candidate mechanism, not a promoted theorem.
