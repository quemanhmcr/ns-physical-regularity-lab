# Hodge strain microscope: let the flow define its own source scale

For every physical radius `r` around a point `x0`, decompose the actual velocity in the ball `B_r(x0)` into its Hodge pieces

`u = h_r + v_r`,

where `h_r=grad phi_r` is harmonic and matches the normal velocity on the sphere, while `v_r.n=0` and carries the local vorticity.

The center strain of the harmonic piece can be read directly from the actual boundary flux. If `n` is the outward unit normal,

`S_h(r) = [15/(8 pi r^3)] integral_{partial B_r} (u.n)(n tensor n - I/3) dA`.

This follows from the degree-two spherical-harmonic projection. It is exact. Translation (degree 1), rigid rotation (zero normal flux), and all higher spherical harmonics do not contaminate the center harmonic strain.

Define the scale-dependent vortical remainder

`S_v(r) = S(x0)-S_h(r)`.

The map `r -> (S_h(r),S_v(r))` is a physical microscope generated by the flow itself. It does not partition Fourier space and does not cut vorticity into arbitrary near/far shells.

A calibration family makes the meaning explicit. For any constant symmetric traceless tensor `S`, in the unit ball define

`v(x) = (1-5|x|^2/3) Sx + (2/3)(x.S.x)x`.

This field is smooth, divergence-free, and tangent to the unit sphere. Its center strain is `S`, but it is self-contained: at the outer boundary the harmonic Hodge component vanishes. On a sub-ball of radius `R`, however,

`v.n = R(1-R^2)(n.S.n)`,

hence the exact microscope profile is

`S_h(R)=(1-R^2)S`,

`S_v(R)=R^2 S`.

Thus at very small radius every smooth strain looks locally potential to leading order, while expanding the observation sphere progressively reveals the surrounding vortical structure that actually supports that strain. The radius at which a prescribed fraction transfers from `S_h` to `S_v` is therefore a source-scale observable rather than an externally imposed scale label.

Combining this microscope with the harmonic energy floor gives, at every radius,

`E(B_r) >= (2pi/15) |S_h(r)|^2 r^5`.

The frontier question becomes: along a hypothetical singular trajectory, how does the Hodge profile move with increasing strain? If finite energy forces `S_h(r)` small at large `r`, the microscope must push the strain-support transition inward, producing a PDE-defined shrinking source scale. At that source scale the strain is genuinely vortical and must be confronted with circulation, vorticity geometry, and viscous direction tax.
