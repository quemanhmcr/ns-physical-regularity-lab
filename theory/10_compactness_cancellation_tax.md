# Compactness-cancellation tax

The multipole ladder has an important qualification.  Higher cancellation is only a stronger remote obstruction when the donor cluster is compact compared with its distance from the target.

Let a localized donor cluster of diameter `L` be observed at distance `d >> L`.  If its net impulse is nonzero, strain begins at order `I/d^4`.  If net impulse cancels, the first surviving impulse moment carries an extra factor of source extent `L`, hence strain is schematically `I L/d^5`.  If the next moment also cancels, it is `I L^2/d^6`, and so on.

Thus after cancellation depth `n`, a compact source pays a propagation factor

`(L/d)^n`.

Equivalently, for a fixed target influence, the required impulse collateral scales as

`(d/L)^n`.

This resolves an apparent loophole.  A donor can avoid the compactness tax by spreading its geometry until `L ~ d`, but then it is no longer a remote compact donor: it has paid in spatial support/nonlocal extent.  Nature offers a trade-off rather than a free route.

The exact closed-ring stack experiment fixes the impulse magnitude of each carrier while shrinking the whole neutral cluster relative to the observation distance.  An opposite-impulse pair should lose influence linearly with compactness; a four-ring stack cancelling both the net impulse and first spatial impulse moment should lose influence quadratically.  The reciprocal collateral needed to maintain a fixed remote velocity should grow as `(d/L)` and `(d/L)^2`, respectively.
