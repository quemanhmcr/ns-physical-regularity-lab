# Arbitrarily delayed viscous visibility of screened radial shapes

The endpoint law generates an ancestry-observer hierarchy

`C_l[D_l^j a]`.

For an even polynomial radial profile

`a(r)=sum_{k=0}^n c_k r^(2k)`,

`D_l` lowers radial degree by two.  The endpoint constraints are triangular: for `j>=1`,

`C_l[D_l^j a]=(l+1)[D_l^(j-1)a(0)-D_l^(j-1)a(1)]`.

Fix `c_n=1`.  One can solve downward so that

`C_l[a]=C_l[D_l a]=...=C_l[D_l^(n-1)a]=0`,

while

`C_l[D_l^n a] != 0`.

Thus the hidden radial kernel can delay its viscous feedback visibility to any prescribed finite order.  This KILLS any argument based on a fixed finite jet of the screened feedback moment.

However, hiding depth is not itself declared a physical cost.  Module 146 also measures two scale-invariant physical quantities:

`R_n = integral r^(2l+2)|a'|^2 / integral r^(2l+2)|a|^2`,

and

`V_n = |C_l[D_l^n a]|^2 / integral r^(2l+2)|a|^2`.

If these grow strongly with hiding depth, the obstruction may come from the radial deformation/diffusion burden needed to keep feedback hidden, not from the artificial polynomial order used to represent it.
