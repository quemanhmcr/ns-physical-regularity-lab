# Forced viscous subscale for null-catalyst amplification

In the critical source branch

`s~tau^-1`, `epsilon~tau^alpha`,

and the linear-null catalyst needed for maintenance has gradient strength

`g~sqrt(s nu)/epsilon^2`.

With `beta=(1+4alpha)/7`,

`g~sqrt(nu) tau^(-7beta/2)`.

At a material vorticity-zero center, Euler cannot change the spectrum of `B=grad omega`. Hence

`g_dot`

must come from `nu grad Delta omega` if the same catalyst is to strengthen.

Define a physical curvature length by

`|grad Delta omega|~g/ell^2`.

Exact balance with

`g_dot/g=(7beta/2)/tau`

gives

`ell^2=2 nu tau/(7beta)`.

Since the source Reynolds is

`R_s=epsilon^2/(nu tau)`,

we obtain

`ell/epsilon=sqrt[2/(7beta R_s)]`.

Thus

`R_s->infinity => ell/epsilon->0`.

The broad null catalyst can be inviscidly reusable, but its **amplification** cannot: keeping the same material catalyst while its strength grows forces a still smaller viscous third-derivative scale beneath the productive core.

The surviving escape is material switching: instead of amplifying one `B` spectrum, the source could continually recruit fresh vorticity zeros that already carry larger gradients. That becomes the next genealogy problem.
